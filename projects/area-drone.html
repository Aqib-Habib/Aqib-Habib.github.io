AREA Intermeshing-Rotor Drone

Development and Validation of a Nonlinear Flight Mechanics Model of the AREA Intermeshing Rotor Drone.

Modelling the aircraft, understanding its dynamics and comparing how three flight controllers respond.

01 / Objective

Two interacting rotors. One coupled flight-dynamics problem.

AREA is an unmanned intermeshing-rotor helicopter. This thesis develops its nonlinear flight mechanics model and assesses the simulation against aircraft flight-test data.

The work connects rotor aerodynamics, rigid-body motion, periodic trim, linear analysis and closed-loop control in a common simulation environment.

02 / Modelling approach

From blade motion to aircraft response.

01

Nonlinear aircraft model

Six-degree-of-freedom rigid-body dynamics coupled to blade-element models with flap and lead-lag degrees of freedom.

02

Rotor interaction

Three-state dynamic inflow for each rotor, with aerodynamic interaction represented through overlapping-disc geometry and prescribed-wake-derived factors.

03

Periodic trim & linearisation

Harmonic-balance trim resolves the periodic body and blade motion over a rotor revolution. Linear models support stability analysis and controller design.

03 / Flight control

Three approaches to stabilising the aircraft.

A cascaded PID controller provides position, attitude, rate and heave control. A stability and control augmentation system (SCAS) uses rate and attitude feedback. A linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) operates on the body states.

Comparisons examine attitude tracking, inter-axis coupling and disturbance response. In the thesis simulations, the cascaded controller restores position after gusts; the LQR limits drift, while SCAS maintains attitude.

04 / Validation & scope

Compare the simulation with the aircraft.

Calibration and assessment use measured rotor power, pitch attitude and longitudinal cyclic across tested airspeeds and rotor speeds. The model captures important hover and level-flight behaviour and supports comparisons between control strategies.

The thesis identifies remaining limitations in full-envelope validity, gust-driven rotor response and high-altitude predictive accuracy. These define the next steps for model development and validation.
